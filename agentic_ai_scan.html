Agentic AI in Finance — Kipkiren Pay
Payment rail exposure, design gaps, and watch items from the global agentic payments signal sweep
🔴 Gap Identified — Act Now

The payment API was designed for humans. Agents are already arriving.

Signal

Ant International open-sourced the Agentic Mobile Protocol (AMP) on 28 April 2026 — the world's first agentic payment framework for mobile wallets, built explicitly because existing payment rails were designed for human-initiated transactions. Fenwick Law notes that 2026 is the year agentic payments move from demos into regulated workflows. The IMF published a formal note in April 2026 on how agentic AI will reshape payment authorisation. This is not a 2027 problem.

Relevance to Kipkiren Pay

The current Kipkiren Pay Rail Contract has no delegation model. An agent acting on behalf of a user — say, an AI shopping assistant in kaLunch automatically reordering a weekly lunch — would need: (a) a credential separate from the user's step-up token, (b) a defined scope of authority (maximum spend, category restriction, frequency cap), and (c) a revocation mechanism. None of these exist in v1.

Add to v1.1 roadmap — agent delegation spec
🔴 Fraud Vector — Immediate Threat

AI-orchestrated synthetic identity fraud: dormant profiles, simultaneous drain

Signal

AI agents are now managing synthetic identity profiles over 18 months, building transaction history to appear legitimate, then executing simultaneous coordinated balance drains across multiple institutions in a single "activation event." Synthetic identities were used in 21% of first-party frauds detected in 2025 (Sumsub). AI-enabled fraud surged 1,210% between Jan–Dec 2025 versus a 195% rise in traditional fraud. Overall fraud losses are projected to rise from $23B in 2025 to $58.3B globally by 2030.

Relevance to Kipkiren Pay

The current rail contract enforces tier limits and rolling-30-day caps, which are rate-limiting controls. But a dormant synthetic identity that has built up a clean transaction history over 18 months would pass those checks. Kipkiren Pay needs cross-account behavioural pattern detection at the platform layer — not just per-account envelope enforcement.

Add to Build Readiness — AI fraud pattern monitoring

Relevance to Kipkiren Pay

CBK does not yet have an agentic AI payments framework — but the EY Global Financial Services Regulatory Outlook 2026 notes AI adoption is "outpacing regulatory oversight" and that human-in-the-loop oversight is becoming a regulatory expectation. Kipkiren Pay's current design has no agent registry, no agent trust rating, and no distinction between human-initiated and agent-initiated transactions in the audit log.

Watch — flag for CBK pre-application meeting agenda
🟡 Watch

AI-driven AML pattern obfuscation: smurfing at machine speed

Signal

AI is enabling automated transactions designed to mimic legitimate patterns, obscure fund origins, and exploit platforms including DeFi and gaming for anonymity. Criminals use AI to structure transactions just below reporting thresholds, coordinate across multiple synthetic accounts, and rotate through platforms faster than rule-based AML systems can flag them. Sumsub and Duane Morris LLP both flag AI-enabled AML as a top 2026 compliance risk.

Relevance to Kipkiren Pay

The current AML approach (tier limits, rolling-30-day caps, POCAMLA/FRC reporting) is rules-based. Rules-based AML is exactly what AI-orchestrated structuring is designed to evade. This is a medium-term concern given Kipkiren Pay's early volume, but the architecture decision (rules-only vs. behavioural + rules) should be made now, not after the platform scales.

Watch — include in AML vendor evaluation criteria
🟢 Design Response

Step-up token model is good — but needs an explicit "agent-initiated" flag

Signal

The CSA Addendum designates identity spoofing and impersonation as Threat T9, and requires organisations to authenticate agents using verifiable credentials with short-lived OAuth 2.0/OIDC tokens. Gartner predicts 30% of enterprises will deploy AI agents acting with minimal human intervention by 2026. The emerging standard (Strata, CSA, IMDA) distinguishes between "subject" (human delegating) and "actor" (agent executing) in every authorised action.

Relevance to Kipkiren Pay

Good news: Kipkiren Pay's step-up JWT already includes audience, subject (Account UUID), and intended_operation claims. Adding an initiated_by claim ("human" | "agent") and an optional agent_id claim to the JWT schema costs almost nothing at v1 and future-proofs the audit log for when the CBK does ask. This is a one-field addition to the Identiti Schema Appendix.

Add to v1.0 Schema Appendix — one claim, future-proof

Agentic AI in Auth / Identity — Identiti
Identity rail exposure to the agentic AI threat landscape — deepfakes, non-human identity, agent delegation, and design gaps
🔴 Critical Gap — Pre-Build

Deepfake-as-a-Service: biometric injection attacks cost $10–50 and bypass liveness checks

Signal

One attacker service recorded 8,065 injection attacks against a single financial institution's liveness checks for KYC in 8 months (Jan–Aug 2025). Deepfake-as-a-Service costs $10–50. The WEF's Cybercrime Atlas (January 2026) tested 17 face-swapping tools and 8 camera injection tools and found most could bypass standard biometric onboarding checks. Palo Alto Networks demonstrated a convincing synthetic identity for video interviews in 70 minutes using consumer hardware. The EU is introducing ISO 25456 and CEN/TS 18099 for injection-attack detection (IAD) specifically because passive liveness detection is no longer sufficient.

Identiti design gap

The Identiti Rail Contract v1.0 specifies "behavioural biometrics SDK" as a Track A dependency and stubs it for v1. This is correct for the full biometric stack. But injection-attack detection (IAD) — specifically blocking virtual camera drivers from feeding synthetic streams into the liveness check pipeline — is not mentioned anywhere in the contract or design spec. IAD is not the same as behavioural biometrics and can be specified as a vendor requirement today.

Add to KYC vendor RFQ — IAD (injection-attack detection) requirement
🔴 Critical Gap — Design

Non-human identity: Identiti has no framework for authenticating AI agents

Signal

Non-human identities outnumber humans 50:1 to 144:1 in enterprise environments (CSA, Strata). Gartner predicts 30% of enterprises deploy agents with minimal human oversight by 2026. The CSA Addendum (Oct 2025) requires a trusted agent registry and short-lived OAuth 2.0/OIDC tokens for agent authentication. The IMDA framework (Jan 2026) lists agent identity as a primary governance dimension. Google's A2A protocol, Anthropic's MCP (OAuth 2.1 added Jan 2026), and Ant's AMP all include agent identity primitives. This is a fast-moving space.

Identiti design gap

Identiti v1.0 issues identities to human platform customers. It has no concept of an agent identity, no agent registry, no agent credential type, and no delegation chain. As KMV apps start embedding AI assistants (a kaLunch ordering assistant, a Chapaa savings advisor), those agents will need to act on behalf of users — and Identiti is the credential authority. The architecture decision on whether agents get first-class Identiti credentials or a separate mechanism needs to be made at v1.1, not discovered under pressure.

Add to v1.1 roadmap — agent identity spec + registry
🔴 Threat — Ongoing

Synthetic identity fraud: AI agents build dormant identities over 18 months to pass KYC

Signal

Synthetic identities combine real and fabricated data to construct a person who never existed. In 2025, 21% of first-party frauds used synthetic identities. AI agents are managing these profiles programmatically, building legitimate transaction history before an activation event. There is often no real victim to report, so detection relies entirely on platform-side pattern recognition. AI-generated documents represented 2% of detected fake docs in 2025 (Sumsub, measured over only 6 months) — expected to grow rapidly.

Identiti design gap

IPRS verification is the primary defence against synthetic KYC at onboarding (confirming the national ID maps to a real person). But IPRS only validates identity at point of onboarding. Post-onboarding, Identiti has no mechanism to detect account activity that looks inconsistent with the declared identity profile (e.g., activity from multiple device fingerprints, unusual step-up patterns, velocity inconsistencies). This is a monitoring gap, not an onboarding gap.

Watch — include continuous monitoring in behavioural biometrics spec
🟡 Watch

OAuth is being rebuilt for agents — the step-up JWT model needs an upgrade path

Signal

Traditional OAuth was designed for humans and static service accounts. The agentic identity landscape is converging on: On-Behalf-Of (OBO) tokens, Token Exchange (RFC 8693), Proof-of-Possession (DPoP), PKCE-mandatory flows, and Continuous Access Evaluation Protocol (CAEP) for real-time revocation. MCP added OAuth 2.1 with mandatory PKCE in January 2026. CSA, IMDA, and major vendors (Strata, Microsoft) are all pointing the same direction. The "subject" (human) / "actor" (agent) distinction in token claims is emerging as the standard.

Relevance to Identiti

Identiti's step-up JWT is RS256-signed with subject, audience, intended_operation, and jti. This is a solid foundation. Adding a actor claim (for agent-initiated flows) and implementing CAEP for real-time step-up token revocation (rather than relying solely on the 5-minute expiry) are targeted upgrades that keep Identiti ahead of the curve without redesigning the architecture.

Add — actor claim + CAEP revocation endpoint to v1.1 roadmap
🟡 Watch

Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM): 80% of recent MFA bypasses use session token theft

Signal

The Microsoft 2025 Digital Defense Report found 80% of recent MFA-bypass breaches used session-token theft via AiTM phishing kits — not credential theft. The attacker proxies the real login flow, intercepts the session token post-authentication, and replays it. This bypasses OTP-based step-up entirely because the valid session token already contains the authenticated claim. AiTM kits targeting M-Pesa-adjacent flows have been observed in East Africa.

Relevance to Identiti

Identiti issues JWTs. A stolen JWT that is still within its freshness window is valid. The current design's defences are: (a) short freshness windows (5 min for step-up, 15 min for phone tokens), and (b) single-use enforcement on step-up tokens via the jti table. Both are good. The gap is the authentication JWT (from POST /v1/auth/authenticate) — what is its TTL? If it is long (hours/days), AiTM interception is much more dangerous. The authentication JWT TTL needs an explicit policy in the contract.

Add — explicit auth JWT TTL policy to Identiti Rail Contract
🟡 Watch

DPA 2019 + agentic AI: the ICO and ODPC are developing AI-specific data protection guidance

Signal

The UK ICO published views on data protection implications of agentic AI in January 2026. The EU AI Act now treats biometric systems as high-risk, requiring transparency documentation. Kenya's ODPC is actively developing its regulatory posture on AI and biometric data — the 2025 registration consultation included questions about automated decision-making. Among the 3,235 enterprise leaders in Deloitte's 2026 State of AI report, 73% cite data privacy as their top AI risk.

Relevance to Identiti

Identiti holds biometric data and makes automated KYC decisions. Under DPA 2019, automated decision-making with significant effects on individuals requires a lawful basis and (in practice) a DPIA. The ODPC registration and DPIA for the biometric module need to address the question: when Identiti's liveness check rejects an onboarding attempt, what is the appeal mechanism? This is a legal design question, not just a technical one.

Watch — flag for data protection counsel engagement

🟡 Watch

Just-in-time (JIT) provisioning is becoming the identity security standard for ephemeral actors

Signal

The CSA Addendum, Strata, and the emerging OAuth 2.1 profile all converge on just-in-time provisioning: create credentials only when needed, bind them to their specific task and delegation chain, and retire them the moment the task is done. No leftover credentials, no risk sprawl. Static service accounts are flagged as the primary risk vector in the CSA's findings — the Salesloft Drift breach was cited as an example of OAuth tokens active months past business justification.

Relevance to Identiti

Identiti's phone token model (15-minute freshness, per-audience) is already very close to JIT provisioning. Step-up tokens (5-minute, single-use) are even tighter. The principle is already embedded in the design. Documenting this explicitly as a JIT identity pattern in the Rail Contract — especially in the B2B pitch — signals design sophistication to investors and enterprise prospects.

Add — document JIT identity posture explicitly in contract language

🔴 Fraud Vector — Active in Kenya

AI-generated smishing: deepfake financial institution SMS bypass conventional spam filters

Signal

KE-CIRT/CC reported 2.5 billion cyber threat events in Kenya in Q1 2025 — a 201.85% increase. Deepfake incidents in Africa surged sevenfold from Q2 to Q4 2024. Commercial anti-smishing tools blocked only 25–35% of threats in 2025. Relevance to Todoku

Todoku manages sender IDs for all KMV apps. A fraudster impersonating a Todoku-managed sender ID ("KaLunch", "Chapaa", etc.) via a third-party aggregator would send smishing messages that appear to come from KMV brands. Todoku's template-only posture protects the outbound channel — but Todoku has no current mechanism for monitoring for sender ID spoofing by third parties, nor for providing consumers a way to verify that a message genuinely originated from the Todoku rail. Sender ID verification is missing.

Add — sender ID spoofing monitoring + consumer verification mechanism
🔴 Gap — Template Abuse Vector

AI-assisted template evasion: approved templates can be exploited as social engineering tools

Signal

The template approval model is a strong control. But a compromised app credential (HMAC secret leaked) allows an attacker to send approved templates to arbitrary recipients — potentially at high volume, using the app's rate limits, with legitimate sender IDs. This is not a novel attack; it is the standard abuse scenario for any compromised messaging API key. With AI-driven recipient targeting (scraped data, enriched contact lists), the damage radius expands dramatically.

Relevance to Todoku

The current Todoku design has HMAC authentication and per-app rate limits. What it does not have is: (a) anomalous recipient pattern detection (e.g., sudden spike in unique recipient rate to previously uncontacted numbers), (b) content-fingerprint monitoring (detecting if an approved template is being used in a pattern inconsistent with the app's normal use), or (c) an API key compromise response playbook beyond "Platform Ops suspends the tenant." These are operational gaps, not architectural ones.

Add — anomalous recipient pattern alert + key compromise playbook
🟡 Watch

AI voice cloning for OTP vishing is a direct attack on Todoku's voice channel

Signal

CrowdStrike reported a 442% rise in vishing in H2 2024. The FBI issued a May 2025 advisory on AI-generated voice messages impersonating officials. ElevenLabs raised $500M Series D at $11B valuation — voice AI is mainstream infrastructure. The specific attack pattern: AI voice call impersonating a financial institution's fraud team, claiming the customer's OTP is needed to "block a fraudulent transaction." High success rate on mobile-first, M-Pesa-native users who are accustomed to receiving calls from banks.

Relevance to Todoku

Todoku's voice OTP channel (TTS readout) is the mechanism being impersonated. Defences are primarily at the Identiti and consumer education layer — Todoku cannot control what calls a fraudster makes. But Todoku can add: (a) a call-back number verification mechanism (the legitimate call comes from a verified number the customer can check), and (b) explicit language in every voice OTP: "KMV will never ask you to repeat this code to anyone." This is a copy and UX decision, not a systems change.

Add — anti-vishing copy to all voice OTP templates
🟡 Watch

AI-driven bulk SMS abuse: agents could hit Todoku's send surface at inhuman volume

Signal

AI agents operating at API speed can exhaust rate limits, probe envelope boundaries, and find the exact threshold below which the diversity check doesn't trigger. The per-second/minute/hour/day rate limits in the current Todoku design are human-scale controls. An agent executing a coordinated blast can stay within each individual window while generating an aggregate volume that would immediately be recognised as abnormal by a human operator — but not by a rule-based envelope checker.

Relevance to Todoku

The existing recipient diversity check (ratio of unique recipients to total messages) is the right control for blast detection. The question is whether the diversity baseline values in the per-app envelope are set tightly enough for agent-speed operations. A human blasting messages accidentally degrades gradually; an agent optimising for maximum throughput while staying under the diversity threshold does it instantly. The diversity check needs a time-windowed velocity component, not just a ratio check.

Add — time-windowed velocity component to diversity check algorithm

🟡 Watch

OTP channel is being specifically targeted — 36% smishing click-through on mobile

Signal

Smishing click-through rates of 19–36% (versus 2–4% for email) make SMS the most effective social engineering channel. OTP-related smishing — fake "your OTP is expiring" or "confirm your payment" messages — are a primary vector because they replicate the exact message format of legitimate transactional SMS. The FBI's IC3 received 22,364 AI-related complaints in 2025, with smishing being the fastest-growing category.

Relevance to Todoku

The OTP-reply inbound channel (where a customer replies with their OTP) is the highest-risk surface. A fraudster who intercepts the OTP by tricking the customer into replying to a spoofed message wins the authentication. Todoku's OTP-reply design correctly limits the reply window (2–30 minutes) and matches replies to the originating message. Adding an explicit "do not share this code with anyone, including KMV staff" line to every OTP template body should be a mandatory template requirement, not optional copy.

Add — mandatory anti-phishing line to all class_0 OTP template bodies

AI agent comms: consuming apps will build AI assistants that need to send messages autonomously

Signal

PayPal acquired Cymbio to enable merchants to accept payments through AI agent interfaces including Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, and ChatGPT. Cross-Rail Implications — Things That Touch All Three Simultaneously

🔗 The initiated_by claim

All three rails should add an optional initiated_by: "human" | "agent" field — to the Kipkiren Pay payment request, the Identiti step-up token JWT, and the Todoku send request. This costs almost nothing at v1. When the CBK, ODPC, or CA-K asks to audit an agent-initiated payment + OTP + notification chain, the cross-rail audit log is already there. Without it, that reconstruction is impossible.

🎭 Deepfake social engineering targets the full payment loop

The highest-risk attack scenario is a coordinated deepfake that: (1) impersonates Identiti/KMV to collect an OTP via voice call (vishing), (2) uses the OTP to pass step-up at Kipkiren Pay, and (3) initiates a payout. Each individual rail has defences for its own surface. The cross-rail response is: explicit "KMV never calls you" copy in every OTP template, every step-up notification, and every payout confirmation — enforced at template approval time, not left to individual app copy.

📊 AI fraud monitoring needs cross-rail signal

Synthetic identity fraud that is dormant across Identiti, Kipkiren Pay, and Todoku simultaneously will not be detected by any single rail's monitoring. The cross-rail Kafka bus already carries identity, payment, and comms events. A platform-layer fraud signal aggregator — consuming events from all three rails — is the right architectural response. This is not a v1 build item, but it should be on the Platform Architecture Overview roadmap so the Kafka topology is designed to support it.

⚖️ Regulatory alignment: "trust-by-design" is the 2026 regulator frame

EY's 2026 Global Financial Services Regulatory Outlook notes that "trust-by-design" controls are becoming gating requirements for real-time money movement globally. CBK's sandbox approach is progressive, but the post-authorisation regime will ask: what controls govern agent-initiated transactions? KMV is ahead of this curve — but should document it explicitly in the CBK pre-application materials rather than treating it as a future problem.

💼 DPA 2019 + agentic AI = new DPIA trigger

When agents make automated decisions about identity verification (Identiti), payment authorisation (Kipkiren Pay), and message delivery (Todoku) without human review, each of those automated decisions may constitute a "solely automated decision with significant effects" under DPA 2019 Section 31. The ODPC registration DPIAs need to address this explicitly — counsel engagement should cover the agentic AI dimension, not just the standard biometric data processing questions.
